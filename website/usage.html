Usage and buyouts, plainly.

your day rate pays for your time. usage pays for what the client does with the work afterwards. two different things, so two lines on your quote.

a five minute read

01

the idea

You sell your time, then you rent out the result.

when you shoot, style or direct, you get paid for the days you work. the photos, the film or the idea that come out of it are still yours. the client gets permission to use them, for a set time, in set places. that permission is usage. a buyout is usage paid in one go.

so a bigger campaign, a longer period or more countries means more usage, even if the shoot day is exactly the same.

02

ask before you quote

Four questions decide the price.

  • Where? social only, website, online ads, print, billboards, tv, in store.
  • How long? three months, one year, two years. "forever" costs the most, if you agree to it at all.
  • Which countries? one country, europe, worldwide.
  • Only them? exclusive means you can't sell the same work to anyone else. that's worth more.

if a client can't answer, quote for what they told you and write down what's not included. they can always buy more later.

03

on your quote

Day rate on one line, usage on the next.

keep them apart, so the client sees what they pay for. a common way to start: usage as a share of your day rate, going up with each of the four answers. small social use might add a little; a national campaign can be worth more than the shoot itself. check what others asked on the questions page before you send it.

a line you can copyusage: organic social and website, the netherlands, 12 months from first use, not exclusive. any other use, or a longer period, by agreement.

the quote maker has a usage line built in, so you don't forget it.

04

when it runs out

After the period ends, the client asks again.

when the usage period is over, the client should stop using the work, or pay to extend it. put a reminder in your calendar for the end date. a friendly mail a month before is often a small, easy extra fee.

05

for ideas, not just images

An idea is work too.

for an art director or creative director, the product is often the idea itself. it's hard to rent out an idea, so agree on two things before you start:

  • what you're paid for: this campaign, this season, this format.
  • what happens if it grows: if the idea turns into a bigger campaign or a series, you get asked first to work on it, or you're paid again.
a line you can copythe concept is made for [campaign], [season]. if it's used again or made bigger, we'd love to be asked first to work on it, at a new fee.
06

watch out

Three words that mean "everything, for free".

  • "all rights" or "full buyout": every use, everywhere, forever. price it like that, or say no.
  • "in perpetuity": forever. ask for a set number of years instead.
  • "work for hire": the client owns the work from the start. common in some countries, rare and worth a lot more in others.

more words like these are in set words.

this is a plain guide, not legal advice. rules differ per country. for a big contract, ask a lawyer or your trade union to read it first.
