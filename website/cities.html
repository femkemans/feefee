Same job, another city.

a day of styling in Amsterdam is not a day of styling in Paris or New York. here's what changes, so you compare like with like.

a four minute read

01

the market

Ask in their city, not only in yours.

rent, the size of the market and what clients are used to paying all differ per city. when a client from another city books you, find out what is normal there before you quote. your own floor still counts: never go below your day rate maker number.

02

hair and make-up

One artist or two.

in the Netherlands, one person often does hair and make-up together. in Paris, London and New York they are usually two artists, each with their own day rate.

  • comparing fees: a hair and make-up fee from Amsterdam covers two jobs. don't hold it next to a make-up fee from New York.
  • quoting abroad: ask whether they need one artist or two, before you say a number. it's in ask first.
03

agents

Who sits in the middle.

in the big fashion cities, many stylists, artists and photographers work through an agency. the agency fee usually comes on top of the creative's rate, paid by the client. in smaller markets, more people work direct.

working without an agent is not a reason to be cheaper. a client used to agency rates expects that number. you just do the agent's work yourself.

04

editorial

Prestige pays little, everywhere.

big magazines in every city pay a small fee, or none, for editorial. people do it for the pictures and the name. if you say yes, agree the days, the shopping budget and the expenses in writing. editorial is cheap, it shouldn't cost you money.

05

working abroad

What to add when you travel.

  • travel days: a day on a train or plane is a day you can't work. put it on the quote.
  • a daily allowance: for food and costs on the road, often called a per diem.
  • tax and vat: inside the EU, a business client often pays the vat themselves (reverse charge). check before you invoice.
  • which law and which currency: say both on the quote. see your terms.
06

the numbers

Ranges per country, then per city.

the fee drop shows real ranges per country, once at least five creatives have shared. as more people share, we split them by city too. never per person, never next to a name.

this is what we hear from creatives in different cities, not a rule. every client and every job is different. what you charge is always your call.
